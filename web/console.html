Before anything sends

Five things gate a live send. The first two will block the software; the rest will only cost you replies.

The deck cover still says “Friends & Family Round”

You are raising an institutional seed. A fund reads that mismatch as a stale deck or a round that failed to fill — and it is the first slide they see.

Postal address is a placeholder

CAN-SPAM requires a real physical address in every commercial email. The footer gate fails on the template string, so nothing can send until it is filled. pitchline profile-set --postal-address "..."

Follow-up updates have no numbers in them

“LOIs in progress” and “waitlist growing” came off the traction slide. A follow-up has to carry genuinely new information, so each one needs a date and a figure — a signed LOI with a named counterparty, a waitlist count, soft-launch orders and repeat rate. Until then the second touch is weak even though it passes every check.

The sending domain is on day zero of its warmup

A new domain gets five sends a day, not forty. That ramp is deliberate: the study this system is built on lost roughly a quarter of its emails to spam filters as volume climbed. Reputation is earned, not configured.

Two credibility markers need a figure attached

“Soft launch confirmed demand” and “LOIs in progress” are your two strongest opening lines with a number, and your two weakest without. Every email below opens with one of your five registered markers — these two are the ones an investor tests first.
